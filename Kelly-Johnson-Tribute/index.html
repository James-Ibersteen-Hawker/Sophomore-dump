Kelly Johnson Tribute

Early Life

Kelly Johnson was born in Ishpeming, Michigan, to Swedish parents. His father ran a construction company. Johnson was 13 years old when he won a prize for his first aircraft design. He graduated from Flint Central High School in 1928, then went to Flint Junior College, now known as Mott Community College, and finally to the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor, where he received a Bachelor's and Master's Degree in Aeronautical Engineering. Kelly received his nickname of "Kelly" when several of his peers decided to call him Clara. He retaliated by tripping one so hard he broke his leg. From then on, he was nicknamed Kelly, originating from the song, "Has Anyone Here Seen Kelly?"
Johnson's work for Lockheed began when he was tasked with conducting wind tunnel tests of the Lockheed Model 10, later the Lockheed Electra. After compeleting his Masters', he joined Lockheed as a tool designer. He was soon promoted to aeronautical engineer after making several changes to the Model 10, and he later became chief research engineer in 1938. He became Vice President of Advanced Development projects (ADP) in 1958.

Life and Legacy

In 1937, Johnson married Althea Louise Young, who worked in Lockheed's accounting department; she died in December 1969. In May 1971, he married his secretary Maryellen Elberta Meade of New York; she died after a long illness on October 13, 1980, aged 46. He married Meade's friend Nancy Powers Horrigan in November 1980. His autobiography, titled Kelly: More Than My Share of it All, was published in 1985. Johnson died at the age of 80 at St. Joseph Medical Center in Burbank

Projects

Lockheed Constellation

Kelly Johnson was the chief designer behind the iconic Lockheed Constellation series, affectionately known as the Connie, or Super-Connie. The Connie was powered by four first piston engines and then later turboprops, with its unique and slender shape writing it a permanent place in aviation history.

Lockheed SR-71

Kelly Johnson was the head of the design team working on the SR-71, the fastest and highest-flying manned jet aircraft ever built. It was retired in October of 1999.

Lockheed C-130 Hercules

Though not directly contributing to the design and construction of the Lockheed C-130 Hercules, Kelly Johnson did play a major role in its coming-about, as he was the man behind the entire organization which created it, Lockheed Skunk Works.
